# Financial incentives and smoking cessation: findings from community settings

Rafael Tanaka, Felix Achterberg, Rafael Quinlan, Priya Mbeki, Hana Albrecht

Published in Annals of Community Medicine, 2012.
doi:10.5555/jace.2012.3841

**Background.** Previous work has examined this question (Sørensen et al., 2005; Ivanova et al., 2006; Bianchi et al., 2002), but evidence remains inconsistent.

**Methods.** We conducted a meta-analysis of randomised trials. A total of 312 participants were included in the analysis. The primary outcome was biochemically verified abstinence.

**Results.** The intervention group showed a clinically meaningful improvement compared with usual care, consistent with earlier reports (Oyelaran et al., 2001; Novotny et al., 2011; Lindgren et al., 2005).

**Conclusions.** The approach is feasible and merits wider evaluation.
