# Financial incentives and smoking cessation: findings from primary care

Hana Albrecht, Siobhan Achterberg, Noor Adeyemi, Siobhan Rahman

Published in Journal of Applied Clinical Evidence, 2021.
doi:10.5555/ghm.2021.2752

**Background.** Previous work has examined this question (Haddad et al., 2005; Ivanova et al., 2017), but evidence remains inconsistent.

**Methods.** We conducted a Delphi consensus study. The analysis included 240 participants. The primary outcome was biochemically verified abstinence.

**Results.** The intervention group showed a clinically meaningful improvement compared with usual care, consistent with earlier reports (World Health Organization et al., n.d.; Adeyemi and Fischer, 2015).

**Conclusions.** The approach is feasible and merits wider evaluation.
